A company style is more than colors and fonts

A brand guide sets the logo, the brand colors and the typeface. A dashboard is where people compare numbers and read change, so it takes many more decisions. Compare two screens built from the same guide.

Fictional brand guide

Orum Foods

  • Primary#D7262E
  • Secondary#F07A3A
  • Navy#1E2B4A

TypefaceSpoqa Han Sans Neo

Both screens use this guide.

1Data color

Colors and fonts onlyBrand red and orange fill the bars, so even rising sales read like a warning. The orange bars also lack contrast on white.

Designed as a styleRed stays in the logo and in bad changes. This year's bars are blue, and last year's step back in gray.

2Change color

Colors and fonts onlyUp is green and down is red, so a falling cost ratio, which is good news, shows red, and a rising return rate shows green.

Designed as a styleColor says whether the change is good for that metric. A falling cost ratio is green and a rising return rate is red.

3Number format

Colors and fonts onlyRaw figures like ₩1,243,512,800 overflow the card, and units as large as the digits leave each value at a different height.

Designed as a styleFigures are compacted to ₩1,244M with smaller units, and each card uses just two type sizes, one for the value and one for the rest.

4Category color

Colors and fonts onlyFive tints of the brand colors fail the color-separation check, so readers keep going back to the legend.

Designed as a styleThree colors plus gray for Other, and only combinations that pass color-vision and contrast checks.

The spec also covers what one screen can't show

  • Screen widths

    How cards fold, from wide monitors down to phones.

  • Korean and English

    Number formats in both languages, and English labels that run longer, in one layout.

  • Filters and cards

    One filter row at the top that scopes every card, and no cards inside cards.

  • Chart choice

    Rules for picking a chart, such as two aligned charts instead of a dual axis.

  • Tooltips and notes

    What a tooltip shows and in what order, and how holidays and events are marked.

  • Accessibility

    Keyboard control, screen-reader tables and reduced motion.

What you get

  • A style name and a spec

    A name your team can ask for, and a written spec (style.md) covering layout, color tokens, type, components, motion and don'ts, so anyone who builds a screen gets the same look.

  • A data color palette

    Categorical, sequential and diverging colors plus good and bad, all checked for color-vision deficiency and contrast.

  • A sample screen

    A sample screen on a topic from your business, switching between Korean and English and laid out for every width.

  • Tool files

    A Tableau color palette (Preferences.tps) with a formatting guide, and CSS variables for web screens.

How it works

  1. Share your materials

    Your brand guide, the dashboards you use now and the metrics you watch.

  2. Review

    We check whether your brand colors work as data colors, and where numbers and charts mislead.

  3. Draft

    We settle the style on one sample screen and name it together.

  4. Hand over

    You get the spec, the palette and the tool files. From then on, ask for it by name.
